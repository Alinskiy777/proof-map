I've been curious about the faceless YouTube niche for a while. There's a lot of advice floating around but most of it comes from people selling courses about it. So I decided to just look at the data myself.  I wrote a scraper, searched 75 keywords related to "faceless YouTube," and collected every video that ranked for them. Then I analyzed descriptions, transcripts, and comments.  Important note: these are videos ABOUT faceless YouTube (tutorials, guides, courses), not faceless channels themselves. So the data shows what the people teaching this stuff are actually doing.  # What I collected  * 1,421 unique videos from 839 channels * 116,214 comments from 100,608 unique people * 1,338 transcripts (3.6 million words total) * \~1,100 full video descriptions * Videos spanning December 2020 to January 2026  After removing 121 videos with 1M+ views (outliers that skew everything), I worked with 1,300 videos.  I also counted unique videos, not raw mentions. So if a tool name appears 50 times in one transcript, that counts as 1 video, not 50.  # The keywords I searched  75 total, grouped roughly into:  * Channel ideas (33 keywords): "faceless youtube channel ideas 2025," "faceless youtube channel ideas for girls," "faceless youtube channel niches," etc. * Automation (12 keywords): "faceless youtube automation," "faceless youtube automation n8n," "faceless youtube automation step by step," etc. * AI tools (12 keywords): "faceless youtube channel ai," "faceless youtube channel ai tools," "faceless youtube channel with free ai tools," etc. * Monetization (10 keywords): "faceless youtube channel monetization," "faceless youtube channels that make money," etc. * Shorts (5 keywords): "faceless youtube shorts," "faceless youtube shorts channel ideas," etc. * General (3 keywords): "faceless youtube," "faceless youtube channel," "faceless youtube channel tutorial"  # Finding 1: What tools creators link vs. what they actually talk about  This was the most interesting finding.  What creators link in descriptions:  * ChatGPT: 163 videos * ElevenLabs: 150 videos * VidIQ: 105 videos * CapCut: 92 videos * N8N: 81 videos  What creators actually discuss in their videos:  * ChatGPT: 284 videos * Canva: 157 videos * Leonardo AI: 57 videos * Sora: 52 videos * Claude: 50 videos * N8N: 30 videos  ElevenLabs appears in 150 descriptions but only 2 transcripts. They link it everywhere but almost never talk about it. That's a pure affiliate play.  Canva is the opposite: 157 transcripts, 63 descriptions. It's what people actually use but the affiliate incentive is lower so they don't push it.  If you're following tool recommendations from these videos, look at what they discuss in the actual video, not what's in the description.  # Finding 2: How these creators monetize  Affiliate programs by unique videos linking them:  * VidIQ: 92 videos * ElevenLabs: 88 videos * Amazon: 60 videos * N8N: 31 videos * InVideo: 22 videos  Course platforms:  * Skool: 109 videos * Whop: 28 videos * Gumroad: 6 videos  Sponsored vs affiliate performance:  * Sponsored videos: 70 videos, 131,682 avg views * Affiliate videos: 208 videos, 98,647 avg views  Sponsored videos get 33% more views. Makes sense. Sponsors vet creators before paying them so the content quality tends to be higher.  # Finding 3: Video length  This one surprised me.  Performance by duration:  * 0-60s (Shorts): 370 videos, 172,250 avg views * 1-3 min: 53 videos, 57,902 avg views * 3-5 min: 49 videos, 23,937 avg views * 5-10 min: 208 videos, 81,173 avg views * 10-20 min: 352 videos, 101,415 avg views * 20-60 min: 163 videos, 113,968 avg views  The 3-5 minute range is the worst performer at 24K avg views. Not short enough to be a Short. Not long enough to be a real tutorial.  Three formats that work: Shorts (172K), long-form 20-60 min (114K), and 60+ min comprehensive videos (123K).  Only 13% of videos in the dataset are 20+ minutes, but they average 114K views. Only 81 mention 1 year.  # Finding 5: What the audience is actually asking for  I scanned all 116,214 comments for buyer intent signals.  * Any buyer intent: 540 videos * Price/budget questions: 366 videos * Alternative seeking: 315 videos * Ready to start: 155 videos * Tool recommendations: 109 videos  The #1 theme in comments is cost. 366 videos have people asking about pricing, complaining about expenses, or looking for cheaper alternatives.
